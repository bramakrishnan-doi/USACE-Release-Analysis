5Baseline Separation (per gage)

Each gage gets its own baseflow construction — pick Diurnal for wastewater/pumping-influenced flows, Flat for steady natural baseflow, Linear Ramp when baseflow differs before vs after the event, or None/Dry for ephemeral reaches that normally carry no flow. Direct storm volume = observed flow minus baseline, floored at zero.

Load gage data in Step 1 to configure baselines.

6Missing Data (gaps) & Fills

Gaps larger than 1.5 × the gage's sampling interval are detected automatically and shaded on the chart in each gage's color. Gaps inside the event window are flagged event. Choose how each gap should be handled — every decision is applied instantly and colored on the chart.

Load gage data in Step 1 to audit gaps.

3Volume & Travel-Time Comparison

Volumes integrate each gage's readings across the event window (1 cfs-day = 1.98347 acre-feet). Ratios compare each gage against the most upstream one; travel times are estimated three independent ways. The bar chart always shows direct storm volume, with acre-feet printed in each bar.

    Routing notes
    Data sources, formulas & method notes

    Data source. USGS Water Data OGC API — GET api.waterdata.usgs.gov/ogcapi/v0/collections/continuous/items?monitoring_location_id=USGS-nnnnnnnn&parameter_code=00060&datetime=START/END, auto-paginating through rel="next" cursor links. Site metadata (name, drainage area, coordinates) from collections/monitoring-locations/items. Values are instantaneous discharge in ft³/s; provisional data may change after review.

    Volume. Trapezoidal integration per reading interval: AF = Q (cfs) × Δt (s) / 43,560; 1 cfs for 24 h = 1.98347 AF. Direct storm volume uses max(0, Q − baseline); total volume uses raw Q.

    Baselines. Diurnal: average flow by time-of-day bucket over N prior days, projected across the event. Flat: mean of all readings in a configurable pre-event window. Linear ramp: mean of the last hours before start to mean of the first hours after end. None: zero baseline (ephemeral). Manual: fixed cfs value.

    Travel time. (1) First rise (leading edge): each gage's first sustained rise above 2% of its peak excess (baseline-removed flow), linearly interpolated between readings and filtered so single-reading noise spikes don't count. Difference between upstream and downstream rise instants = the operational warning/lead time — when the release first begins to show downstream, ahead of the crest. If a gage is already above threshold when the window opens, its rise is flagged "already elevated at window start." (2) Peak-to-peak: event-window crest time difference — when the peak itself arrives. (3) Cross-correlation: best lag maximizing similarity between the two normalized excess hydrographs (search up to the configured max, on a uniform grid at the finer of the two gage intervals). Centroid delay — the volume-weighted center of mass of the excess hydrograph, i.e. the instant half the routed volume has passed — is still computed as an internal cross-check and exported to CSV, but is omitted from the operations view because it reflects average mass transit, not actionable arrival times. Wave speed uses straight-line gage separation (HAVERSINE) against the first positive of peak-to-peak → cross-correlation → centroid — actual river distance is longer, so treat speeds as upper bounds.

    Downstream arrival forecast. Calibrated from the event: initial abstraction Ia is the source direct volume released before the downstream first rise; conveyance efficiency Ceff = Varrived / (Vreleased − Ia); front lag is downstream minus upstream first rise; crest lag is downstream minus upstream peak time; peak attenuation α is downstream peak excess ÷ upstream peak excess; recession decay k is fitted log-linearly to the downstream falling limb. The proposed source hydrograph is time-warped (source first rise→front arrival, source peak→crest arrival), attenuated by α, smoothed with a Gaussian kernel (σ = clamp(0.15/k, 2, 12) h), given an exponential bank-storage tail, and normalized numerically so the hourly series integrates to VDome = max(0, Ceff × (Vproposed − Ia) × Φseasonality × Φantecedent). Tuning changes only affect the forecast, never the observed-event results in Step 3 or the baseline calibration in Step 5.

    Gap fills & bias. Linear interpolation is the neutral default. Hold last biases volume slightly high during recession gaps; fill with zero biases low; scale from upstream transfers the adjacent upstream gage's flow scaled by the drainage-area ratio (only when both areas are published) — best for instrument outages. Unfilled gaps are excluded from integration and flagged; the excluded share of the event window is reported in the data-quality column.

    Timezone. Dialogs and chart axis show your browser's local timezone; underlying data are UTC instants from USGS.

    Not for design or permitting decisions without independent verification. Provisional USGS data are subject to revision.
